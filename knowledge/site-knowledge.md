Variables To Consider When Choosing A Coffee Supplier

Choosing a coffee supplier is just like calibrating a new bag of coffee, there are so many variables to consider and oftentimes, it becomes overwhelming. While this list isn’t exhaustive, here are some important variables to consider when choosing a supplier that will have the most impact on your coffee business.

1. Quality of Coffee Beans:

Ensure your supplier provides high-quality, freshly roasted beans that is free of any major defects. Defects can include severe insect damage, moldy beans, and foreign bodies like small stones that will ruin your grinder. If you don’t have a grinder, some suppliers will grind upon request. If that is the case, make sure it is sealed properly and consumed immediately upon opening because ground coffee stales significantly faster than whole coffee bean. Check out the standards set by reputable organizations such as the Specialty Coffee Association, Coffee Quality Institute, and ASEAN.

Ask your supplier about their standard operating procedures and quality-checking practices. Check for relevant qualifications, licenses, certificates, and accreditations. If your supplier isn’t a direct manufacturer or roastery, you may be paying higher prices for questionable quality coffee.

Your coffee will need to rest for a few days up to a month from roasting, depending on the coffee. A good roastery will always provide the roast date and recommended rest period. Avoid suppliers that do not provide roast dates. Remember, it is better to purchase coffees that are freshly roasted and let them rest in your shop rather than to risk buying old stock that is long past its peak.

2. Coffee Options:

Choose suppliers offering a diverse selection of blends and single-origin beans. Attend coffee cupping events to find the best matches for your café and customers. High-quality coffees have distinct origin characteristics that will stand out in your brew if they are roasted right. Go to these events with a goal and taste profile in mind but also be open to new experiences – a lot of specialty coffees will surprise you!

You’ll presented with so many options to choose from, but you don’t have to buy all the blends and origins that the supplier offers. Taste and see what’s available and choose the coffee that best suits your café and your customers’ preferences. There’s no need to buy the super expensive, rare origin coffee if the market you cater to has yet to discover the world of specialty coffee!

3. Customer Service:

Find a coffee supplier that is willing to go the extra mile to meet your coffee needs. Your supplier should be responsive, helpful, and willing to find ways to accommodate your requests, ensuring timely deliveries and addressing any concerns you have about the coffee. Your coffee supplier should be able to help you calibrate your equipment to get the best out of your coffee. Some will even go as far as helping you develop your recipes and menu.

Be wary of suppliers that are eager to sell you their coffees but are hard to contact after making the sale, leaving you wondering when your coffee purchase will arrive at your shop, or if it will arrive at all. You work hard to give the best quality to your customers, it’s only right to expect the same high-quality service from your supplier.

Coffee tasting notes are like a map to a world of flavors waiting to be explored. Understanding these tasting notes can enhance your coffee experience and deepen your appreciation for the brew.

When we talk about tasting coffee notes, we're referring to the range of flavors and aromas that can be found in a cup of coffee. Just like wine tasting, coffee tasting involves identifying and describing these flavors to capture the essence of each brew.

Mindful tasting can add a new dimension to your daily cup. It's about moving beyond the simple distinction of "strong" or "weak" coffee and discovering a world of nuances. You might start noticing hints of fruitiness, chocolatey undertones, or floral aromas with different coffees and roast levels.

Whether you prefer a bright and acidic coffee or a rich and velvety blend, knowing the tasting notes can guide your selection.

### Coffee Extraction Theory: Overview
- URL: https://puregroundscoffee.com/blogs/pgccoguidetocoffeeexcellence/coffee-extraction-theory-overview

Ever wondered why some cups of coffee taste incredibly smooth and balanced, while others are overwhelmingly bitter or sour? The answer lies in coffee extraction—the process of dissolving coffee compounds into water.

Why Extraction Matters

When you brew coffee, water will extract flavor compounds at different rates and different amounts throughout the whole brew. The right balance leads to a well-rounded cup, but an imbalance results in:

Under-extraction: Coffee tastes too sour, and sharp, because not enough sugars and bitter compounds have dissolved.

Over-extraction: Coffee is too bitter and dry, as water has pulled out undesirable compounds.

What Is Extracted From Coffee?

Coffee is a complex beverage made up of hundreds of naturally occurring compounds that shape its flavor, aroma, and texture.

Salts contribute to balance and enhance other flavors, while acids—such as citric, malic, and phosphoric—create brightness and vibrance in the cup. Sugars, including sucrose and other simple carbohydrates, add natural sweetness and roundness. Oils, like cafestol and kahweol, carry aromatic compounds and contribute to body and richness, especially in unfiltered brews. Complex carbohydrates such as galactomannans and arabinogalactans give coffee its smooth texture and mouthfeel, making every sip fuller and more satisfying.Only 27-35% of ground coffee is soluble, . The target extraction is only about 18-22% of the coffee.

### Coffee-to-Water Ratio for Better Extraction
- URL: https://puregroundscoffee.com/blogs/pgccoguidetocoffeeexcellence/coffee-to-water-ratios-for-better-extraction

Are You Using Too Much Water in Your Brew?

If you’ve ever felt that your coffee tastes flat, watery, or inconsistent—your coffee-to-water ratio might be the reason. While many baristas and café owners focus on equipment or beans (and rightly so), the ratio between coffee and water is one of the most crucial variables in achieving a consistently excellent cup.

In this article, we’ll break down the importance of brew ratios, answer common questions café owners ask, and offer simple steps to elevate your coffee game.

What Is a Coffee-to-Water Ratio?

Simply put, it’s the ratio of the amount of ground coffee to the amount of water used in brewing.

The standard measurement is written like this:

1:15 or 1:18

Which means:

1 gram of coffee for every 15–18 grams (or mL) of water.

Why It Matters:

• A smaller ratio (e.g., 1:15) means stronger coffee because there’s more coffee in less water.

• A larger ratio (e.g., 1:18) results in lighter, more diluted coffee.

What’s the Best Coffee-to-Water Ratio?

There’s no one-size-fits-all answer, but the specialty coffee industry generally agrees on a starting point known as the Golden Ratio:

1:15 to 1:18

This works well for most manual brew methods like pour-over, AeroPress, French press, and batch brewers.

Start here, then adjust based on your:

• Brew method

• Coffee Roast level

• Customer preferences

• Grind size and brew time

What Happens When You Use Too Much Water?

Using too much water doesn’t just make your coffee taste weak—it also wastes your beans.

• You’re diluting flavor without necessarily extracting more of the good stuff.

• You’re giving customers a poor experience.

• You’re reducing the perceived value of your cup—even if you use high-quality beans.

Remember:

We’re trying to caffeinate, not hydrate.

(But yes, hydration is still important.)

Formula to Get It Right Every Time

Dose × Brew Ratio = Brew Water

Where:

• Dose = amount of ground coffee (in grams)

• Brew Ratio = your chosen multiplier (15, 16, 17, 18, etc.)

• Brew Water = total water (in grams or mL)

Example:

20g of coffee × 15 = 300g of brew water

Pro Tip:

1 gram of water = 1 mLIsn’t the metric system beautiful?

How Can I Keep My Coffee Consistent?

1. Use a Scale

Eyeballing it won’t cut it. Even a 2g variation in coffee dose can shift the entire flavor profile. A digital scale ensures consistency across all baristas and shifts.

2. Train Your Team

Make sure everyone on your team understands extraction theory. Knowing the “why” helps them execute the “how” more precisely.

3. Dial in Based on Feedback

Use the Golden Ratio as a starting point. Get customer feedback and adjust as needed.

Why Does Coffee Extraction Matter to Café Owners?

Because consistency = customer loyalty.

If your brew tastes different every day, your customers notice—even if they don’t always know why.

Better extraction means:

• Fewer complaints

• More repeat orders

• A stronger brand identity

• More efficient use of coffee stock

TL;DR for Café Owners:

• Start with a 1:15–1:18 coffee-to-water ratio.

• Use a scale to stay consistent.

• Train your baristas in the basics of extraction theory.

• Use data (like brew ratio) to make better brewing decisions.

• Stronger doesn’t always mean better—better means balanced.

### Grind Size Explained: Too Coarse or Too Fine?
- URL: https://puregroundscoffee.com/blogs/pgccoguidetocoffeeexcellence/grind-size-explained-too-coarse-or-too-fine

If your coffee tastes too sour, bitter, or just plain weak, the problem might not be your beans — it might be your grind size. Whether you’re a home brewer, barista, or café owner, understanding grind size is one of the most important steps to brewing better coffee.

In this guide, we’ll break down what grind size is, how it affects extraction, and how to choose the right grind for every brewing method.

What Is Coffee Grind Size?

Grind size refers to how fine or coarse your coffee beans are ground. This determines how much surface area is exposed to water, and how quickly water flows through the coffee bed.

Finer grind = more surface area, slower flow, more extraction

Coarser grind = less surface area, faster flow, less extraction

Getting the grind right is essential for proper extraction — the process of pulling soluble flavors from ground coffee. Grind too fine or too coarse, and you'll either over-extract or under-extract your coffee.

Why Grind Size Matters

Grind size directly affects:

1. Extraction Yield

Finer grinds extract more because water stays in contact with more surface area. Coarser grinds extract less, which can lead to weak or sour coffee if contact time is too short.

2. Flow Rate

In percolation methods (like espresso or pour-over), grind size controls how quickly water passes through the coffee. A grind that’s too fine can clog the filter and slow everything down; too coarse, and water rushes through too fast.

3. Flavor Balance

Grind size influences which flavor compounds are extracted. A well-matched grind size will give you sweetness, clarity, body, and balance. A mismatched grind can make even the best beans taste bad.

Common Grind Size Mistakes

Using one grind size for all brewing methods

Not adjusting grind based on brew time or method

Relying on pre-ground coffee instead of grinding fresh

Ignoring environmental factors like humidity or bean age

How To Know If Your Grind Size Is Off

Sour or watery? Your grind may be too coarse → under-extracted

### How Does Brew Time Affect Coffee Flavor?
- URL: https://puregroundscoffee.com/blogs/pgccoguidetocoffeeexcellence/how-does-brew-time-affect-coffee-flavor

Ever wonder if coffee gets pressured when you're timing it?Or is it okay to leave it brewing for as long as you want?

In coffee extraction theory, timing isn’t just a suggestion—it’s critical to how your coffee turns out in the cup. Let's break it down.

What Is Brew Time in Coffee?

Brew time is the total time your water is in contact with your coffee grounds.It’s one of the most important brewing variables, affecting what, and how much, flavor gets extracted from your coffee.

Depending on your brewing method, brew time can either depend on other parameters—like grind size and pouring technique—or it can be fixed, like in immersion methods.

For example:

In pour-over brewing, your brew time is influenced by grind size, water amount, and your pouring style.

In French press, the brew time is usually set—typically around 4 minutes—before you press the plunger and serve.

How Brew Time Affects Coffee Extraction

Brew time directly impacts the extraction level of your coffee.

Shorter brew times extract less and can lead to under-extraction.This means your coffee might taste sour, overly acidic, or flat, because not enough flavor compounds were extracted.

Longer brew times extract more and can lead to over-extraction.This can make your coffee taste bitter, astringent, or harsh—because too much was extracted, including undesirable compounds.

Finding the Sweet Spot

The goal?Find the perfect brew time for your brewing method and recipe to unlock the full flavor potential of your coffee beans.

Different brewing methods have different ideal brew times:

Pour-over: Typically 2.5 to 4 minutes, depending on recipe and grind size.

French press: Around 4 minutes steep time.

Espresso: 20 to 30 seconds extraction window.
